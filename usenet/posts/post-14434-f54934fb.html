esjeje1@aol.com (Esjeje1) wrote:

>To paraphrase TK (on the principles of history), if a change is made that is
>large enough to alter permanently a long-term trend, you cannot predict the
>consequences of such changes. I think this applies here. The natural system of
>genetics is surely a long term trend, as old as the earth itself.

Historically, the consequences of any such change are just as likely
to be good as be bad. If a change turns out to be bad, we generally
know about it very quickly and can alter course (the massive early
coal-smoke pollution from the "black satanic mills" of the early
Industrial Revolution, for example. In this way man continues the
eons-long process of evolution, with his own intellect substituting
for natural selection.

Therefore, I maintain that in the long run all technological change is
good.

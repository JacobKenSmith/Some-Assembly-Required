Ted's take on morality is a bit confusing.

> 25.  The moral code of our society is so demanding that no one can think, feel
> and act in a completely moral way.
>   For example, we are not supposed to hate anyone, yet almost everyone hates
>   somebody at some time or other, whether he admits it to himself or not.

I'm not sure. Perhaps in the 50's there was a time when people were
never supposed to say anything bad about anyone. Particularly, women
were socialized to always be "nice". Now in the 90's people have
cultivated a culture of "sanctioned agression" (TV's favorite phrase is
"I'm gonna kick your ass!")

It is true that one cannot express "hate" along ethnic lines. LIkewise
among what Ted would call "leftists" and I would call "wishy-washy New
Agers", people can't express a "negative vibration". LIkewise in the
born-again Christian subculture, one simply can't express hate; one has
to put on a front of "forgiveness" and pray to be "delivered" from the
bad feelings.
So the question of hate/anger is far more complex than Ted's analysis.

>   Some people are so highly socialized that the attempt to think, feel and act
>   morally imposes a severe burden on them.
>   In order to avoid feelings of guilt, they continually have to deceive
>   themselves about their own motives and find moral explanations for feelings
>   and actions that in reality have a nonmoral origin.
>   We use the term "oversocialized" to describe such people.

This is definitely true among born again Christians & other spiritual
groups. I speak as a survivor of the former group!! In "mainstream"
society, one goes to a shrink to deal with guilt.
I wish Ted would elaborate on "feelings and actions that have a nonmoral
origin". Which feelings are those? Just about everything has a moral
basis. Unless he is talking about the simple struggle to survive. 

Morality is incredibly complex. A vegetarian decides that it is immoral
to buy into the worldwide factory farming of animals by consuming their
product. This person can still get plenty of food from the local market.
But a mountain hermit can shoot a squirrel to keep from starving & that
isn't an immoral act.

> 
> 26.  Oversocialization can lead to low self-esteem, a sense of powerlessness,
> defeatism, guilt, etc.
>   One of the most important means by which our society socializes children is
>   by making them feel ashamed of behavior or speech that is contrary to
>   society's expectations.
>   If this is overdone, or if a particular child is especially susceptible to
>   such feelings, he ends by feeling ashamed of HIMSELF.
>   Moreover the thought and the behavior of the oversocialized person are more
>   restricted by society's expectations than are those of the lightly
>   socialized person.
>   The majority of people engage in a significant amount of naughty behavior.
>   They lie, they commit petty thefts, they break traffic laws, they goof off
>   at work, they hate someone, they say spiteful things or they use some
>   underhanded trick to get ahead of the other guy.
>   The oversocialized person cannot do these things, or if he does do them he
>   generates in himself a sense of shame and self-hatred.
>   The oversocialized person cannot even experience, without guilt, thoughts
>   or feelings that are contrary to the accepted morality; he cannot think
>   "unclean" thoughts.
>   And socialization is not just a matter of morality; we are socialized to
>   conform to many norms of behavior that do not fall under the heading of
>   morality.
>   Thus the oversocialized person is kept on a psychological leash and spends
>   his life running on rails that society has laid down for him.
>   In many oversocialized people this results in a sense of constraint and
>   powerlessness that can be a severe hardship.
>   We suggest that oversocialization is among the more serious cruelties that
>   human beings inflict on one another.

I think the bigger problem isn't "morality", so much as simple
constraint on our every day lives. Society constrains us by the fact
that we are forced to spend an hour in a car every day, forced to spend
1/3 of our salary on insurance, forced to spend every day in a dank
office. And when we go home we are unable to find release in peaceful
natural surroundings, because the developers have destroyed every
vestige of nature and forced us to live in a concrete maze. We also
can't relax by going on a walk & meeting friends, because our culture
has so situated us physically & socially that such simple/natural
behavior is impossible.

So in theory we are very "free" in this society--free to write what we
want, free to open our own business or buy what we want, free to go to
the church of our choice, etc. But in reality we feel like rats running
on treadmills & have no clue how to get off.

The fact that we know we are "free" in theory causes us to be even more
frustrated and out of touch with our feelings; if we are dissatisfied,
we wonder "what's wrong with me??" and think of all those poor refugees
who would give anything to live as we do. And then we add "guilt" to the
rest of our frustration. Finally we can't stand it anymore & dull our
feelings by watching TV.

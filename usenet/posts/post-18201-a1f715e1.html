  1. What is the purpose of this newsgroup?
   
   The newsgroup alt.fan.unabomber exists for "fans of the mystical and
   awe-inspiring UNABOMBER" to discuss matters related to topics
   mentioned in its charter, including FC, their Manifesto, and
   revolution.
   
   This newsgroup name reflects the media mangling of a single FBI
   suspect who has eluded them for over 17 years and is possibly a member
   of the revolutionary group FC, which has an unknown number of members.
   The FBI suspect known as UNABOM was dubbed "The Unabomber" by the
   media because the media can sell more advertising for their newspapers
   and television programs when they inflate a story, introduce fictional
   elements, and provide a oversimplified one-dimensional perspective
   that misses the meaning of the event entirely. Since they are
   interested in providing entertainment for ratings and care little
   about provoking thought or seeking truth, the media will do everything
   possible to increase their ratings and persuade audiences to depend on
   their viewpoints.
   
   For the remainder of this FAQ, we will refer to "The Unabomber" as FC,
   for the group that he belongs to. This group authored the Manifesto
   entitled Industrial_Society_and_its_Future that will be referred to
   throughout this FAQ. While the suspect is unknown, the group has
   claimed responsibility for the actions that the FBI has attributed to
   this single individual.
   
   This group does not exist to:
   
       a) Condemn the actions of FC because of their supposed lack of
       contributions to society. Their Manifesto is a work of sheer
       genius that may prove to be the force that will save humanity from
       its devolution.
       
       b) Condemn the actions of FC because they did not conform to the
       arbitrary values of a passive society.
       
       c) Spread media propaganda about "Unabomber suspects," though the
       method of propaganda used by the media can be discussed and
       exposed.
  

  2. What does FC stand for?
  
   FBI officials for a long while suspected that the initials FC that
   were carved on FC's bombs stood for "Fuck Computers" but this is
   incorrect. The FBI later realized that FC has explained that the
   initials stood for "Freedom Club" in one of many letters that they had
   sent.
   
   It is unknown how many people are members of FC, but some have
   suggested that thousands of people individually share the basic ideas
   expressed in the Manifesto and are working towards similar goals. Some
   people have suggested that revolutionaries working toward the same
   goals as FC should consider themselves part of the organization.
   
   
  3. You are a strong supporter of FC. Are you a fed?
   
   I am not a fed. I am an interested ex-scholar who believes in the
   right to free speech, and in exercising my constitutional right I
   often side with the ideas of FC and defend FC against foolish
   criticisms. However, the threat of feds tying to entrap people is very
   real, so I suggest several guidelines for the safety of FC fans,
   members, and others who are disgusted by recent social trends and wish
   to spread new, more intelligent values. This information is intended
   mostly for audiences in the United States because so far there has
   been less interest in FC and their Manifesto from other areas of the
   world.
   
       a) Do not openly organize revolutionary efforts on the Internet. All
       Internet traffic is monitored at the major hubs by the government
       (mae-east, mae-west, fixed-east, and fixed-west have been
       confirmed as fully monitored and logged and newer net hubs are
       likely the same way. Assume that all email is intercepted and
       logged forever.
       
       b) Public posts are also logged, and not only by the government
       (search for your name on USENET archives kept at
       http://www.dejanews.com and http://altavista.digital.com if
       you've posted articles previously). If a current or potential
       employer runs your name through one of these services and doesn't
       like your opinions, you might run into problems.
       
       c) PGP is ok for exchanging basic information, but the computing
       power of the future will allow brute force attacks in a decade or
       two. If you use it, assume that your message will be read by
       others at some point in the future. Also make sure to use a
       2048-bit key or larger if you have a hacked version of the PGP
       software.
       
       d) To further the revolution, it is best that you work alone or
       with people that you have known a long time and can trust with
       your life. Do not work with someone you found on the Internet
       because they could be feds trying to entrap you, no matter how
       much you think you could trust them. Even exchanging ideas with
       another person could lead to implicating you in crimes that you
       are innocent of, so be careful even when discussing ideas with
       others.
       
       e) "Anonymous" remailers aren't necessarily anonymous and can't
       always be trusted. Since the feds can monitor all major network
       connections, they can see when you use a service like the one at
       anon.penet.fi because your message goes through at least one of
       their hubs on its way to Finland and it is trivial for them to see
       your message with full headers before it reached the anonymous
       service. Better methods are one-way chains of remailers if you
       want to post to USENET or send a fellow revolutionary advice.
       
   For more information on anonymity and cryptography you can read the
   Cypherpunks page at
   
   http://nic.funet.fi/pub/unix/security/crypt/cypherpunks/Home.html
   
   You may also want to read newsgroups such as alt.privacy,
   alt.privacy.anon-server, alt.privacy.clipper, and
   comp.society.privacy.
   
   If you are comfortable with the feds monitoring you and feel that this
   cause is important enough that you should participate in the
   discussion, then it is ok to use your name, but be warned of the
   possible dangers. Just be sure to refrain from offering information
   which is too specific or could implicate you in anything.
   
   
  4. If you are so against technology then why do you use the Internet?
   
   This question appears almost every week and seems to come only from
   people who have not read the Manifesto and understand it only
   superficially from what the media has told them about it. The short
   answer is that we are using the Internet to communicate with other
   revolutionaries and to spread the message of FC to others who are
   disenfranchised with the strangle hold of the system. We agree that
   technology is powerful, especially methods of communication, and we
   are using these powerful technologies solely as weapons to weaken and
   ultimately destroy the system.
   
   As FC wrote in the Manifesto:
   
   202. It would be hopeless for revolutionaries to try to attack the
       system without using SOME modern technology. If nothing else they
       must use the communications media to spread their message. But
       they should use modern technology for ONE purpose: to attack the
       technological system.
       
   
  5. You seem to like nature, protecting the environment, and supporting free
  speech while disliking pollution, domination of society by business
  interests, and conformity to arbitrary societal values. Are the fans and
  supporters of FC really leftists?
   
   Fans and supporters of FC rarely identify with one of the two
   mainstream parties. Most of us dislike the leftists and see them as
   harmful for many reasons, including their "feelings of inferiority"
   and "oversocialization." Paragraphs 6 - 32 and 213 - 230 discuss FC's
   position on this in detail.
   
   "Political correctness" has been a disaster for society because it has
   eliminated certain ideas from being acceptable for discussion. The
   left has decided that it didn't want people to think and communicate
   about things it doesn't like and declared that these ideas were
   improper. The left is only for free speech when it is not dominant and
   is rapidly fascist when it is dominant. This alone makes the leftist
   movement unacceptable to anyone who supports freedom.
   
   The leftist of old had several appealing ideas when they were not
   dominant, but they turned out to be worse than their opponents when
   they gain power.
   
   It is safe to assume that the vast majority of FC fans and supporters
   are not leftists.
   
   
  6. You seem to like rationality, fairly obtained success, and fighting for
  justice while disliking weakness, inferiority, and collectivism. Are the fans
  and supporters of FC really conservatives?
   
   Fans and supporters of FC rarely identify with one of the two
   mainstream parties. Most of us dislike modern conservatives and see
   them as harmful for many reasons, including their forced submission of
   individual rights to business rights, blind support of "progress" and
   the technoindustrial system, and dogma towards old, antiquated methods
   for dealing with new problems they have inflicted on society. While
   these topics are addressed throughout the Manifesto, there is a
   paragraph directed at the conservatives.
   
   50. The conservatives are fools: They whine about the decay of
       traditional values, yet they enthusiastically support
       technological progress and economic growth. Apparently it never
       occurs to them that you can't make rapid, drastic changes in the
       technology and the economy of a society without causing rapid
       changes in all other aspects of the society as well, and that such
       rapid changes inevitably break down traditional values.
       
   We note that conservatism used to mean individual freedom and limited
   government control over people's lives. While modern conservatism in
   the United States includes a good deal of government control over
   freedoms, including the prohibition of "consensual crimes" and forced
   acceptance of governmental morals (both of which are largely based on
   Christian roots), it also creates an environment where business
   dictates how government is run. This means that the proletariat are
   merely cogs in the machine who can have their freedoms removed if it
   would serve business better. For example, look at the hysteria of the
   drug war. While educated people understand the parallels with the
   first Prohibition (when was the last time you heard of someone killed
   over alcohol - when it was illegal) and that most drug users keep to
   themselves, the great fear from business is that people will use drugs
   and will lose interest in their mindless jobs and not be as productive
   as possible.
   
   Conservatives claim to be for less government control and less
   interference with people's private affairs but these claims are only
   true as long as people are doing things that are in accordance with
   the conservative's values. As soon as people start wanting these
   freedoms for their individual rights instead of as business rights (or
   as human machines for business), they find that this great
   conservative promise of freedom does not apply to them.
   
   It is safe to assume that the vast majority of FC fans and supporters
   are not conservatives.
   
   
  7. If you're not left-wing or right-wing then what is your political
  position? Don't you have to be one or the other?
   
   Most of the fans of FC don't buy the idea of a two-party dictatorship
   wherein all citizens are supposed to believe in the ideas of one of
   the two parties. We find this concept to be far too limiting. We also
   find both parties to be in error in most of their decisions. However,
   the political process of appealing to herd values is largely to blame
   for this. Politicians are generally not interested in addressing
   legitimate problems and prefer instead to do whatever will gain them
   the most public support. There are few politicians who have genuine
   concern over the problems they face and for that reason and their
   general ineptness and stupidity, we oppose the two-party system they
   have created.
   
   Some supporters of FC are libertarians, though because of their pledge
   to never impose force on other people, they oppose FC's occasionally
   violent methods. Apparently is never occurs to them that revolution is
   often accompanied by violence because the oppressors who are the root
   causes of social stresses will not relinquish their positions of power
   without being violently overthrown by the proletariat. But because
   these people are interested in similar reforms to gain autonomy we
   gladly work with them, though some of the supporters of FC are not at
   all interested in political positions beyond reducing the amount of
   government control in our lives.
   
   Some supporters of FC are members of the Green party, which is most
   active in environmental issues. While the danger of dealing with
   leftist is possible, the benefit of being involved with this sort of
   party is that you can meet many enlightened individuals who are
   interested in achieving the same goals. The position on technology
   also varies with some believing that technology is the only way to fix
   the damage inflicted by the technoindustrial system and others
   thinking that widespread support for Green issues will allow nature to
   heal itself.
   
   
  8. Dr. Ted Kaczynski wasn't a productive citizen of society. So why listen to
  him?
  
   [Dr. Kaczynski is the latest FBI suspect in the "Unabomber crimes" but
   this in no way means that he was the Unabomber, a member of FC, or the
   author of the Manifesto.]
   
   This question most frequently is asked by people who have been
   oversocialized and believe that everyone must serve society in some
   way. These people believe that everyone should perform their 40-hour
   work weeks involved in a surrogate activity so that workers can earn
   money to provide for their nuclear family while making the company
   owner more wealthy in the process. People who make this argument do
   not seem to have great tolerance for others who aren't eager to
   conform to this model of societal behavior.
   
   The most obvious response is that there is no law that someone must be
   a "productive" citizen and spend the majority of their waking hours
   working. Wouldn't it make more sense to do what you wanted to do all
   day instead of what your boss wanted? The relevance of Dr. Kaczynski
   to this discussion is that he was a hermit who lived on very little
   money and spent his time expanding his intellect instead of doing
   pointless work for a company. This was his choice for a lifestyle,
   which he adopted after resigning from being a Berkeley professor.
   Since there is no reason to expect others to work for your society,
   there is no basis for disliking someone for their lack of contribution
   to it in the manner that you would prefer.
   
   Historically, many authors and artists have dropped out from the
   working world so they can dedicate themselves to developing their
   skills and producing work which often pays little or nothing and is
   not recognized as genius until after their deaths. People today who
   have great spirits often find themselves beaten down by the stress of
   modern work environments and society and are forced to make a
   difficult decision between letting their natural abilities fade or
   quitting their jobs to tap the energies and talents of their
   abilities.
   
   If Dr. Kaczynski is the author of the Manifesto, it could be said that
   he was much more productive than most citizens because of his ability
   to start a dialog about important topics and inspire revolutionaries
   to wage war against a broken system that is held together with
   delusions and empty promises. If he is the author, then we owe him a
   great deal of gratitude and respect for his work.
   
   
  9. What is the connection between FC and environmental groups like Earth
  First?
   
   While FC has strong environmental concerns, they have no link to
   groups like Earth First. While many fans and supporters of FC support
   the efforts of environmental groups such as Earth First, these groups
   are largely composed of leftist collectivists and are opposed for this
   reason.
   
   214. [...] A movement that exalts nature and opposes technology must
       take a resolutely anti-leftist stance and must avoid all
       collaborations with leftists. Leftism is in the long run
       inconsistent with wild nature, with human freedom and with the
       elimination of modern technology. Leftism is collectivist: it
       seeks to bind together the entire world (both nature and the human
       race) into a unified whole. But this implies management of nature
       and of human life by organized society, and it requires advanced
       technology. You can't have a united world without rapid
       transportation and communication, you can't make all people love
       one another without sophisticated psychological techniques, you
       can't have a "planned society" without the necessary technological
       base. Above all, leftism is drive by the need for power, and the
       leftist seeks power on a collective basis, through identification
       with a mass movement or an organization. Leftism is unlikely to
       ever give up technology, because technology is too valuable a
       source of collective power.
   
   For this reason, Earth First is an organization to avoid though on the
   surface it appears to share a few positions with FC.

  11. Don't you think FC should be ignored for resorting to violence to make
  themselves heard?
  
   The answer to this question requires the discussion of several related
   topics. For the first section we will quote FC from the Manifesto.
   
   96. [...] Anyone who has a little money can have something printed, or
       can distribute it on the Internet or in some such way, but what he
       has to say will be swamped by the vast volume of material put out
       by the media, hence it will have no practical effect. To make an
       impression on society with words is therefore almost impossible
       for most individuals and small groups. Take us (FC) for example.
       If we had never done anything violent and had submitted the
       present writings to a publisher, they probably would not have been
       accepted. If they had been accepted and published, they probably
       would not have attracted many readers, because it's more fun to
       watch the entertainment put out by the media than to read a sober
       essay. Even if these writings had had many readers, most of these
       readers would soon have forgotten what they had read as their
       minds were flooded by the mass of material to which the media
       expose them. In order to get our message before the public with
       some chance of making a lasting impression, we've had to kill
       people.
       
   Some oversocialized people will say that violence is wrong and that
   using freedom of speech is the best way to change problems in our
   society. However, this is flawed because few people today are willing
   to listen to intelligent, reasoned conversation and either hold
   tightly to their own preconceived notions about the way society should
   function and what individual rights are deserved. Others are
   uninterested in discussion of a rational nature because thinking is
   either difficult for them or causes boredom because of withdrawal from
   the entertainment media stimulus they are accustomed to. While people
   can certainly freely discuss their views, others rarely listen or
   respond to them, so freedom of speech is often of no use. In the
   spirit of being "open-minded" others simply disregard opinions with
   which they do not already agree.
   
   Since authority has traditionally used violence to keep others down,
   it seems fair that violence can be used against them to gain back
   freedom and autonomy.
   
   FC's dedication to their beliefs is certainly admirable in a time when
   few people stand for anything consistently. This is especially true
   when our society is exerting continuous pressure to conform, be
   passive, and refrain from confronting threats to one's lifestyle or
   freedom. While we are forced to walk the plank a step away from pure
   apathy, groups like FC who stand up for freedom are should be
   supported. FC's reasoning can be summarized in the following
   approximation: actions produce change, talk produces no change.
  

  12. What do you revolutionaries plan to do exactly?
  
   Most of us who are committed to changing the harm we see in society
   are not going to disclose our plans, but our intentions are fairly
   obvious. You might read about our work in newspapers and maybe your
   grandchildren will read about them in history books, but you'll
   probably see little offered in the newsgroup about our plans because
   that would be harmful to the revolution. A basic overview of some
   methods we will use is available in paragraphs 180 - 206.

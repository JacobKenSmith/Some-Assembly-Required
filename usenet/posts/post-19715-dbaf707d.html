You Wrote:
>some would argue that technology is what destabilizes society, 
>they would say that you can't make rapid, drastic changes in the
>technology and the economy of a society without causing rapid changes
>in all other aspects of the society as well, and that such rapid
>changes inevitably break down traditional values.

You make an excellent point and one with much merrit. Let us assume that 
a society is very conservative in it's values and closed to new ideas. 
Perhaps it has a political power structure similar to the War Lords of 
Japan. Would not such a society be more fragile and susceptible to 
destablizing factors than a pragmatic and dyamic society that is vibrant 
and open to opportunity? I offer Japan in the early 1900's as only one 
example. Perhaps you can think of others, more recent or current. 

One might be led to determine that societies, not unlike people cannot be 
generalized when considering the effects of drastic change. I would offer 
that it is not so much technology which alters society, but technologies 
effect on the quality of life of a society. To the extent that technology 
alters nature, one might contend that it alters society. To the extent 
that it destroys nature, it destroys the quality of life for all mankind. 
To the extent that it enhances nature, it enhances the quality of life of 
all mankind.

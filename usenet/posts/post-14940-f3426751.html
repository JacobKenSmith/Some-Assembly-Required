Here's a summary of what's  really going on in the trial.  
Others have touched on this a bit:

++Warning: This discussion posits, for sake of arguement only,
that TK is the unabomer, a charge for which he has not been convicted.


The government's main concern in the unabomer case is to prevent
large numbers of people from espousing or even knowing about
his anti-tech and especially his anti-corporate views.  At 
first the best way to do this seemed obvious: kill him.
Hence the death penalty, with the procecution about to go to
great lengths to prove he's sane.

Then, once he asked to be his own lawyer, the gov't. realized 
that he could try to justify his actions by pointing out the
evils of tech. and  big corps.  (the defence advocated by
attorney Serra)  This would mean that every day CNN, NYT,
etc. would have to talk about all the bad things big corps.
have done...such a scenario is unacceptable for the govt.
Hence the fake suicide, and the new willingness to accept
a plea bargain.  They now figure he'll get less media attention
in jail his whole life (where he might be killed or suicided
anyway) than the attention he gets during the (monthlong?,
yearlong?) trial.  They will say they don't want a "circus
atmosphere" but what the don't want is a daily moral indictment 
of Exxon, GE, GM, etc.

Given the above, TK will face an interesting decision once he
is found competent: Should he accept the plea bargain?  According
to the Manifesto (#168.)

       Besides, we all have to die some                            
       time, and it may be better to die fighting for survival,
       or for a cause, than to live a long but empty and
purposeless            life. 


Hence he may feel that a life-or-death case will draw more attention
to his ideas than any prolific writings he may be able to produce
in jail.  What would you do if you were Ted?

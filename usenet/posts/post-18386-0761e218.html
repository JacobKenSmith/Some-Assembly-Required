How did this lead to him bombing people and things?

TK differs from most people in the value he places on human life.  To
him it seems, there is not inherent meaning in a human life.  

He apparently has massive trouble (or non-interest?) in communicating
with people.  (Does anyone have a sense of how isolated his life in
Lincoln was?  I get the sense, that it might not be too bad.  Did he
see random townspeople on a daily basis?  Or did he go days without
flexing his vocal cords?)  In any case, regardless of how isolated it
was... it was probably considerably more so than the life most people
are interested in living.  I don't think TK wanted this life.  The
American dream.  A wife and kids that understood what went on in his
head, maybe, but not a huge number of relations...  If there was such
a thing as a Freedom Club, it seems TK would have voted by absentee
ballot if possible.

He had all these ideas going 'round in his head and no one to talk
about them with.  So he wrote letters.  Lots of letters.  More than
most people probably -- by far.  But would he have enjoyed regular
conversation with these people if they lived in Lincoln?  I don't get
the sense that he would have.  Letter writing is one-sided and is much
different than "right-there" communication.  Can pen-pals be
considered friends?  I don't think so.  It seems the writing is more
for the benefit of the writer than the recipient.

For TK, Simply maximazing the "total happiness" on the planet by more
and more people that are slightly less and less happy is not worth it.
It would be better to have fewer people, but have them truly "happy"
ala "the power process" -- whatever that means.  (As far as I can
tell, it means that an individual is either directly responsible for
providing food and shelter for himself, or else they personally know
the individual that is.)

So what is the meaning of life for TK?  Serve God?  Enjoy friends and
family?  Accumulate "stuff".  I don't think any of these would be Ted's
answer.  But what would?

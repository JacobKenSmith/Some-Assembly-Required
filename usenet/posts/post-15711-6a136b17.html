USA Today commentator Linda Chavez once suggested that a "less talented
radical environmentalist" sent the bomb to Paul A. Rosenberg in December
1993.  We've since seen that the bomb used the same 9 volt and 6" pipe as
the Unabomber's June 1993 bombs.
Was his area of research something that concerned Ted?

As background, consider that Ted had taken notes about a Dr. Yudofsky, who
was quoted in "Can Science Cure Crime" in the March 1995 Scientific
American.  He is a research psychiatrist interested in the neural
substructure of human aggression.  According to a published account, the
FBI called Dr. Yudofsky, and told him that his name was in Ted's cabin,
(and many others), along with annotations to his article.

You may also remember Professor McConnell, who although most famous for
his work in popularizing behavior modification, was a pioneer in this area
of neuropsychiatry and the control of criminal behavior.

Was Dr. Paul A. Rosenberg, who was doing important research work at
Harvard in attempting cures for diseases ranging from Parkinson's to
Alzheimer's,  pursuing research that would upset Ted?    He was studying,
for example, how excitatory amino acids serve as a common pathway for
neurologic disorders.  (See March 1994 New England Journal of Medicine.) 
Maybe even related to drug addiction.

	The Unabomber (Ted) wrote:  "But the fact that human thoughts and
feelings are so open to biologic intervention shows that the problem of
controlling human behavior is mainly a technical problem; a problem of
neurons, hormones and complex molecules; the kind of problem that is
accessible to scientific attack."

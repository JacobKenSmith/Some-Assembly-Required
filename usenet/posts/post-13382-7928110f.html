Paragraph 7, "But what is leftism? During the first half of the 20th century
leftism could have been practically identified as socialism. Today the movement
is more fragmented and it is not clear who can properly be called a
leftist...What we are trying to get at in discussing leftism is not so much a
movement or an idealogy as a psychological type..."

Paragraph 28, "...the left takes an accepted moral principle, adopts it as its
own, and then accuses mainstream society of violating that principle..."

Paragraph 29, "Here is an illustration of the way in which the oversocialized
leftist shows his real attachment to the conventional attitudes of our society
while pretending to be in rebellion against it..."

Paragraph 191, "One should think twice before encouraging any other social
conflict than that between the power holding elite (which wields technology)
and the general public (over which technology exerts its power)..."

I think that TK is using an untraditional definition of leftism that is very
different than the definition others here are using. Maybe he should have
coined a new word for his concept. The leftist, as defined by TK is attached to
the system, and is accusatory of the public; the revolutionary is supposed to
fight against the system without alienating the public.

Being concerned about the environment or the social problems described in the
manifesto does not automatically make one a leftist. Expecting society to do
something about it, or giving up your own power to a collective could qualify
you as such.

TK wasn't too kind to the conservatives either, which most people associate
with the right. He did call them fools, in paragraph 50, after all.

I'm not sure how political most people are - they are mainly concerned about
their own lives, not about the "right" or "left". TK wrote about things that
have provoked a great deal of personal soul searching, and that supercedes any
political label that one might attach to me or other such supporters. In this,
I don't think I'm unique.

Hi Renate,

Yes, you are right, Renate.  He approaches leftism "not so 
much a movement but rather a collection of related types."  
But that's common for extreme rightists, including those 
who are anarchistic.  

Kaczynski writes:
"When we speak of leftists in this article we have in mind 
mainly socialists, collectivists, 'politically correct' types, 
feminists, gay and disability activits, animal rights activists 
and the like."  (para. 7)

If you fall within these groups, he doesn't want your support --
he urges that his supporters not collaborate with such types.
Among EF!ers, he wants the support only of those who are
"thoroughly unleftist."  (para. 227)

His hatred of liberals is why he didn't get along with his 
parents.  They were traditional liberals.

I agree with Jorn that the manifesto is not rambling.  The
extensive arguments about leftists, however, struck many
commentators (like Kirkpatrick Sale) as totally unnecessary
to his Luddite argument.  Maybe it is -- maybe it isn't.  His
manifesto isn't just about technology:  it's more broadly
about his conception of freedom.

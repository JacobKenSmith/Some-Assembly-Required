WILD nature and wood

FC writes:
> "The positive ideal that we propose is Nature. That is, WILD
> nature: those aspects of the functioning of the Earth and its
> living things that are independent of human management and
> free of human interference and control."

I don't buy it.  At least not in the manifesto or the 1971 essay.

(1971 => "In these pages it is argued that continued scientific and
technical progress will inevitably result in the extinction of
individual liberty."  Humans again... not nature.)

> "And with wild nature we include human nature, by which we
> mean those aspects of the functioning of the human individual
> that are not subject to regulation by organized society but
> are products of chance, or free will, or God (depending on
> your religious or philosophical opinions)."

Ah, this is more like it.  This is what TK cares about.  *HIS*
life.  Human nature.  Crack the puzzle of his own life.

He must of personally been a big fan of wood, as we know.  But it is
just amazing that he was so passionate about certain things.  I mean,
he must have known he would eventually get caught.  (Though using
bombs rather than guns would make it easier to be in denial about
this.  Removed from the real world.  Life with people.  A family.)
Somehow he claims to care about this abstract idea of humanity SO
MUCH, that he is willing to take a few individual lives.  I'm don't
think I buy it.  I mean, who are the individual lives that he
cherishes in his own life?  His brother's?  His parents'?  His
"pen-pal"/"friend" in Mexico?

Why?  Where does this strength of emotion for "things" and "ideas"
come from?  Why not people instead?  There is no way one can claim to
care about humanity, and not "be" with people on a regular basis.  Is
there?  I think not.  He's a misanthrope.  That's for sure.

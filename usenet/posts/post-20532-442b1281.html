> This starts poorly. Not only it is mainly obfuscation, but he states a
> series of personal opinions as fact, not a particularly strong foundation
> to build his case upon. To claim that our current condition of unhappiness
> and angst is the direct result of the industrial revolution is to assume
> that there was a happier time somewhere in our past. History says
> otherwise. Would Dr. Kaczynski be as happy as a Russian peasant serf under
> the Tzars, crippled by hard labor and dead at 35 from anthrax? Or as a
> Zulu warrior (an agrarian hunter-gatherer society, stone-age technology)
> where the murder rate was 10 times modern levels and the battles raged for
> decades? Or be a medival peasant and get run through by a barbaian's blade
> smeared with pig shit? In fact, to claim that the present level of the
> world's fortunes is *lower* than it has been in the past is sheer
> arrogance--it is insulting to the people of the past, who have *really*
> suffered.

> THE UNUCRITIC

True, old forms of GOVERNMENT were oppressive, class segregated, etc.  But
revolutions and new governments are not dependent on technology.  Modern
democracy could afford us all life, liberty, and the pursuit of happiness
with or without light bulbs, television sets, and SCUD missiles.  My
grandparents went without alot of the conveniences I have today, and yet
they tell me the world used to be a better place than it is today.  Hell,
maybe if we weren't all so concerned about TV's and cellular phones and
bigger and better weapons, we could feed and shelter the poor and the
homeless, and talk to our neighbors instead of clicking our garage door
openers from the safety of our cars, sanitarily shutting out everything
that doesn't come into our house on cables.  

Don't get me wrong, I love my computer.  I love the internet.  But I'd
give it all up to make the world a better place.  Wouldn't you?

The system does not and cannot exist to satisfy human needs. Instead, it
is human behavior that has to be modified to fit the needs of the
system. This has nothing to do with the political or social ideology
that may pretend to guide the technological system. It is the fault of
technology, because the system is guided not by ideology but by
technical necessity. Of course the system does satisfy many human needs,
but generally speaking it does this only to the extent that it is to
the advantage of the system to do it. It is the needs of the system
that are paramount, not those of the human being. For example, the
system provides people with food because the system couldn't function
if everyone starved; it attends to people's psychological needs
whenever it can CONVENIENTLY do so, because it couldn't function if too
many people became depressed or rebellious.

-- The Unabomber Manifesto; Paragraph 119

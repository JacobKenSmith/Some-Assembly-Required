From what I've read:

Japan essentially took control of Vietnam over from France (who were
under German control at this point) during World War II.  At that time
the Viet Minh was organized from the nationalist movements that had
sprung up because of massive discontent during French rule
("protection" is what the French called it). The nationalists were
prepared to seize power when Japan was defeated by America.  The Viet
Minh took over North Vietnam, and also invaded South Vietnam (there
has already been a North and South for a long, long time).  The French
fought them, drove them out of the South, and both parties tried to
negotiate a settlement.  This attempt failed, and war broke out.

The French had US aid, and were powerful enough to to force the Viet
Minh into adopting guerrilla warfare tactics.  Dien Bien Phu was a
turning point, and France finally agreed to negotiations.  This was in
1954.  Both sides were to have elections in 1956. Diem came into power
in the South, and never held elections.

So where did you read about elections being held in the South?

The moral authority for our government to take life comes from the
democratic majority of course. Each member of the majority of society

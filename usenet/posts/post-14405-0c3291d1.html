If you revisit paragraph 196, "...It will be easier to destroy the industrial
system on a worldwide basis if the world economy is so unified that its
breakdown in any one major nation will lead to its breakdown in all
industrialized nations."

Enter the IMF, with its goal of stabalizing industrial economies. Congress is
about to debate funding the IMF with an additional $20 Billion US dollars. The
mainstream reporting is that if this isn't done, the effects of collapses in
other countries who are both our customers and our competitors would damage our
own economy. Various groups want to attach provisions to any US IMF fundings -
environmental policies, labor or human rights provisions, and other groups,
though of course, those which will be taken most seriously are the economic
interests. The corporate world is quite anxious that the IMF funding be passed
through Congress. Interesting enough, several Congressmen are not willing, due
to the above mentioned concerns. (I don't have their names, I just listen to
NPR, which I personally find to be the least biased source of news, when I feel
like listening to news at all.)

Meanwhile, I seem to have read somewhere that the Pope is encouraging poor
third world countries not to repay their IMF loans (which actually serve to
cripple those countries more, due to the large interest payments, and which
cause environmental degradation amongst other things.) The Pope is also
critical not only of communism, but of capitalism - he's not too happy with
what's going on in Poland. If anyone has more info on these subjects, I would
be interested.

Couple this with the "Year 2000" problem (and recently, the Washington Post
reported that there is a serious lack of programmers. I imagine there is a lack
of COBOL or other "legacy" (old programming languages) programmers to solve
this problem, which supposedly will bring down government, banking and other
industry systems.) The Year 2000 problem will start affecting people before the
year 2000, because the fiscal year will start in 1999. 

So, I am wondering, what is everyone on this newsgroup prepared to do? I would
welcome the collapse of the IMF, because I think it is a ruthless organization.
We can't predict the outcome of its collapse though.

This is just the sort of breakdown discussed in the "manifesto".

The members of this newsgroup have certainly represented the ideals in
paragraph 187 "On the more sophisticated level, the ideology should address
itself to people who are intelligent, thoughtful and rational. The object
should be to create a core of people who will be opposed to the industrial
system on a rational, thought-out basis, with full appreciation of the problems
and ambiguities involved, and of the price that has to be paid for getting rid
of the system. It is particularly important to attract people of this type, as
they are capable people and will be instrumental in influencing others."

I know I have done my small part in spreading the message, and will continue to
do so.

So, now we have a chance to make a difference. The future predicted in Ted's
work may be closer than we thought. His work was quite timely indeed. Perhaps a
little too close shaved for some.

What are we going to do about it?

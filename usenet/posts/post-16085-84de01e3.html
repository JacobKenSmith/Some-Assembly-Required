An example of how pervasive is the characterization of the Manifesto as
"crazy": I went to buy it at the bookstore (before I knew I could get it
on the Internet) and the guy at the bookstore check-out had the nerve to
sneer at me like "you're reading THAT trash?" I should'a clocked him
one. You NEVER insult what a customer reads. and what are the chances
that the guy actually read it??? he probably opened it at random, took
one look at the page & shut it.

Which is not to say that the Manifesto doesn't have problems. It starts
off strongly in the first few sentences, but then begins an unfortunate
digression into anti-Leftist stuff. It then goes on a bit too long about
"surrogate activities" , the "power process", etc. Which are important
topics, but he belabors the point. It doesn't really pick up steam
upntil about P.111 ("Industrial society cannot be reformed".), I think.
>From then on, FC's fiery conviction reaches out & grabs you by the
throat. I recall reading it & wanting to jump up & down & go "yeah!
right on! He's right!"

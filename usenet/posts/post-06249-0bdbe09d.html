Scott,

A very perceptive and on target piece, as usual. I see that Agent really
couldn't argue with you, except for a little dig about your "overanalysing." I
guess that's because Agent is not as familiar with ISAIF as you or any of the
regulars here.

I daresay that TK would be glad of Scott's analysis, which has always been
thoughtful. See paragraph 187, "On the more sophisticated level the ideology
should address itself to people who are intelligent, thoughtful and rational."

I would also cite TK's letter to Dr. Tom Tyler of UC Berekeley, a psychology
professor to whom he sent a copy of ISAIF before it was published in the
Washington Post. TK "asked Tyler to think seriously about the substance of the
issues involved. And he asked him to consider a number of questions, such as:
"Do you think we are likely to be right about the future that technology is
creating for the human race? If you think we are wrong, then why do you think
so? ... If you think we have identified some present social problems correctly,
do you think anything can be done about them? Will they get better or worse
with continued growth or progress?""

see http://www.unabombertrial.com/archive/1995/070595.html

This is what a.f.u. has been about for a long time, a discussion of this
question, which TK evidently considered important, or he wouldn't have done
this.

"Para. 229 is as close as TK ever gets to a 
straightforward definition of leftism, and it's 
a good one.  It certainly works for the leftists
in this forum."

Agent is overly fond of paragraph 229, but it's not as strong as he would like
us to believe. "Anyone who strongly sympathizes with ALL of these movements is
almost certainly a leftist." The emphasis on ALL is TK's and I would still note
his allowing that some folks might agree with all of the items iterated (the
term PC or politically correct is *not* used here, btw) and still not be a
leftist.

In fact, I wonder how many leftists of the psychological type we have in the
regulars on this forum. It's true that we have a number of people with
political inclinations leaning to the left, as well as some conservatives.
Agent and Pyro's only "proof" that the regulars of a.f.u. are PC'ers or
leftists (under TK's definition) is that we disagree with them.

I would direct Agent to paragraph 230, "The more dangerous leftists, that is,
those who are most power hungry, are often characterized by arrogance or by a
dogmatic approach to ideology..." Folks, keeping in mind that leftism as
defined by TK is a psychological type, who does that remind us of?

"As for his contempt for leftist totalitarian
tendencies, and leftist moralizing, that's too
obvious to bother proving.  It shines forth in
any honest reading of his work, either ISAIF or
"Ship of Fools"."

I wonder if Agent has read TK's 1971 essay? This essay was the foundation of
ISAIF, and was about *technology*!

"The real question is how anyone could come to doubt it."

No one doubts TK's dislike of psychological leftists, which may include
political leftists and may also include political rightists. In fact, TK seems
to identify himself as a libertarian in his 1971 essay, and later as an
anarchist in ISAIF, so no doubt that Scott's analysis that TK might be a
libertarian anarchist in a recent post was correct. I for one, would like to
know more about this.

In ISAIF, TK was advocating the destruction of technology. I really have no
doubt that his anger towards leftists as he defined them had everything to do
with the failure of the anti-technology movement in the '60's, which was in
fact leftist.

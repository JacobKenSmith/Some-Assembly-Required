flipper@griffin.co.uk (Fingers McPhee) wrote:

>I am really interested on people's views as to why the Unabomber is a
>net icon (or is he? Is it just me who thinks that?).

  It seems that the streotypical 'computer geeks' are paradoxically
the ones that seem to be the Unabomber's biggest fans.  Could this be
because the manifesto strikes such a responsive chord with those that
are alienated from nature the most -- the people that sit in front of
a computer screen ten or twelve hours a day?

   Here are people that derive great monetary and material wealth in
using and exploiting one of technology's greatest successes.  They
often spend most their waking hours in postmodern MUDS and MOOS.
These artificial constructs of reality don't seem to offer the same
sort of spitual or psychological nutrition that RL ('real life' in CS
terms) does.

 In our society, Gonzales said, "War is OK. Abortion is OK. Killing
  animals is OK. We just give it a different name and don't really
think about it too much. The Unabomber just had his own war going on."

  I have a feeling (or is it wishful thinking that i am unwilling to
acknowledge as such?) that further separation from nature by society
will produce more Unabombers.

The avid Internet and/or computer addict (again, i am speaking in
generalizations) has a minimum amount of contact (and in many cases
actively shuns) wild places, 'nature' - in the sense of undeveloped,
or minimally developed, areas.  "Outdoor recreation", to the addict,
is an oxymoron.

Computer labs at universities are filled with this type of creature.
_Homo_ _virtualis_; a pasty-skinned organism that is seldom seen
outside of it's chosen habitat of sterile, florescent lit cubicles.
While the physical delineation between it and it's symbiont (the VDT,
keyboard, and console) is often easily determined, the psychological
and philosophical boundries are much more difficult to map out.

_H. virtualis_ often withdraws from friends, job, family, and what i
broadly call "Nature" -- choosing instead a reality filled with
avatars, VR, and 'emoticons'.  THAT is where the 'cut off from Nature'
part comes in.

So, to spell it out...  The paradox is that those individuals that
have embraced such a powerful, and often dehumanizing technology, are
enamored with their ultimate iconoclast.  To the Unabomber, such
technology results in the loss of individual autonomy; the
de-evolution of the once-proud human race into a culture of consumers
obsessed with the latest gadgetry at the expense of culture, Nature,
beauty, and freedom.  As is written in section 129...

"Technology repeatedly forces freedom to take a step back -- short of
the overthrow of the whole technological system." 

The Unabomber also directly attacks the 'computer elite' numerous
times, particularly in Section 147 and 173.

So in conclusion, here is an individual whose actions and words
_directly_ threatens and declares war on the Social Construct of
Reality formed by those that most enthusiastically push this new
technology onto the populace -- you can't even pick up a news magazine
anymore without the "Information Superhighway" being described in
glowing terms by those most intimately involved with it.

You would _think_ then, that the computer elite would simply dismiss
the Unabomber as some sort of unenlightened Luddite.  Instead,
alt.fan.unabomber is a thriving Usenet group; apparently close to 3000
Web sites are devoted to Unabom; and many hackers and programmers i
know in my local community often spend precious modem time discussing
the subtle aspects of the Manifesto.

_This_, my friends, is the paradox of which i speak.

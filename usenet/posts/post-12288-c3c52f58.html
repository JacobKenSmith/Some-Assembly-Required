TK in ISAIF made several references to addictions, including addiction to
technology. I think this may be at the heart of the surrogate activities
section.

In paragraph 39, he writes: "...Given a person who devotes much time and energy
to the pursuit of goal X, ask yourself this: If he had to devote most of his
time and energy to satisfying his biological needs, and if that effort required
him to use his physical and mental facilities in a varied and interesting way,
would he feel seriously deprived because he did not attain goal X? If the
answer is no, then the person's pursuit of a goal X is a surrogate activity."

And of course, it follows that if the answer is yes, then it is not a surrogate
activity.

Now, it's not up to TK to define our surrogate activities for us. He did
ascribe surrogate motives to scientists, and perhaps this was from his own
experience, and perhaps the motives of scientists was a faulty section of the
manifesto. I don't want to end up there - I would like to talk about this
point, if you are willing. I think he raised a valid point in the surrogate
activities section; we might be conscious of those indicators.

I have some thoughts about addiction: if you engage in an activity to seek
refuge from pain in life, and go back to that activity to hide from the pain,
with lesser effect and greater frequency, then it is an addiction. If you
pursue an activity because it brings out the life in you, and not as a means to
hide from life, but because you feel joyful during and afterwards, then it is
not an addiction.

Now, I feel that people in the modern world are faced with so many addictive
activities, not just the obvious mind altering ones, that one may not even
realize that one is addicted. When one doesn't have realization, how can this
be other than a surrogate activity?

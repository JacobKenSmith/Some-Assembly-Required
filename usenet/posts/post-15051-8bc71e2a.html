Re: The Unabomber Case

Dear Mr. Denvir and Ms. Clarke,

I have been following the Unabomber case since Theodore Kaczynski was
arrested. I hope you will not mind if I offer some unsolicited advice.

Presently the defense seeks to highlight Mr. K.'s mental problems. This
is a good approach as far as it goes, but I believe there are other
factors which could help the jury understand and empathize with Mr. K.

If Kaczynski fears technological society as "an omnipotent
organization"... then his fears are only a few degrees more intense than
those of many other people in this society. Are there not many people
who fear rampant destruction of the ecosystem, genetic engineering, the
dehumanization of a mass society, and artificial intelligence? 

You may or may not be aware that the Unabomber has many supporters.
There is an internet newsgroup, alt.fan.unabomber, on which we have been
discussing the issues for a year and a half. By and large these are not
"kooks" who advocate bombing and killing... these are thoughtful,
intelligent people who are concerned about the future of this planet. We
empathize with the Unabomber's concern, even if we don't support his
methods.

I believe that this support factor is something the defense team should
tap into. My advice is to use passages of the Manifesto as testimony
that "he may have been mentally disturbed... but his fears are, to some
extent, legitimate." Would this not help to gain jury sympathy? I
certainly think it preferable to the tactic of citing the nasty things
Mr. K. said to his parents as a way of trying to prove that he is
"sick". (which,  by the way, does not make the jury like him.)

Ask the jury whether they could not agree with statements such as these
quotes from the Manifesto:

"Modern man is strapped down by a network of rules and regulations, and
his fate depends on the actions of persons remote from him whose
decisions he cannot influence." (para.114)

"Freedom and technological progress are incompatible." (p.113)

"Man in the future will be no longer a creation of Nature..but a
manufactured product."(p.122)

"The average man's fate is no longer in his own hands...but in those of
politicians, corporations, executives and remote, anonymous
technicians." (p.128)

"Technology has gotten the human race into a fix from which there is no
easy escape."(p.170)

"Those who are employed...will have to be ever more reliable, conforming
and docile, because they will be cells of a giant organism." (p.175)

I suggest you study the Manifesto closely and urge the jurors to view it
as more than "the ravings of a schizophrenic". The prosecution is using
Mr. K's writings against him. I suggest your team review the Manifesto
and use it in his defense. The defense needs all the resources it can
get.

This would not necessarily detract from your efforts to highlight Mr.
Kaczynski's mental disturbance. It would simply help jurors to
understand that "he may have problems, but in some ways he is similar to
us." 

Thank you for listening to my unsolicited opinion, and let me close by
congratulating your team on what you have already done and wishing you
the best of success in the future.

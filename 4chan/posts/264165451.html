Here is a preliminary reply to your question anon, please say where you think it may be wrong/insufficient:

>He argues that tech will end up causing environmental harm in the future that none of us today could even imagine 
Just take into account -just as an illustrative example- the terrible harm automobiles have caused: 
In 1890, NO ONE except for Mr. Ford himself could have foreseen this, and even Ford didn't in fact foresee all that amount of harm that millions of automobiles would cause (paragraphs 126 and 127 of ISAIF, and the first sentence of paragraph 128).
Yet just a few decades later, -at least in the industrialized countries- a citizen could not live 'normally' without owning and using a car. Kind of like today in relation with smartphones, social media, etc.
The whole 'smartphone situation' is a great example of a situation that no one would have been able to predict of future harm, yet it still happened, without anyone planning it or foreseeing it. 
>and yet he proclaims that we should not bother ourselves in the matters of people in 500 to 1,000 years in the future 
Ted seems to imply that anons should not bother themselves about what may or may not happen 500 years in the future IF the anti-tech revolution is succesful. That paragraph seems to be in the text (ISAIF) only to support the necessity of anti-tech revolutio here and now more than any other reason.
And Ted is probably right -once again- about ignoring the distant future: total collapse will bring with it many more pressing and important problems for anons that survive collapse other that ''what will happen in 500 years''. This is probably why Ted feels comfortable saying ''don't worry about shit that may or may not happen in 500 years.''

Hope this helps, please say so if it does not.
